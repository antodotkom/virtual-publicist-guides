Message Playlist Curators Directly (Video and Notes)

Most advice about playlists starts with a form and ends with silence. This 49-second episode from Virtual Publicist shows a different route: an independent artist finds one specific curator, writes to her directly, and the conversation goes somewhere. The notes below walk through the episode and pull out what an artist can copy for their own release.

What happens in the episode

The artist opens the Independent Spotify Playlist Curators collection, a group of curators who run their own playlists and are open to hearing new music. Instead of sending the same note to everyone, the artist picks one curator whose playlist fits the song and sends her a message.

The curator listens over coffee. She asks for an unlisted link, so she can hear the track properly before it goes public, and then adds the song to that week's update of her playlist, Late Light. It is a small story, but every step in it is one an artist can control.

Three things worth copying

Choose the curator before you write the message. The episode works because the artist picked a playlist where the song belongs. Listen to a curator's recent additions first. If your song would sound out of place between them, move on to the next name.

Have the unlisted link ready. The curator's first reply is a practical request. Artists who have to go and find a private link, or who send a download that expires, lose momentum. Keep a private streaming link, the release date and a one-line description of the song in one place before you message anyone.

Know the curator's schedule. Late Light is updated weekly, so the song lands in "this week's" update. Many independent curators refresh on a fixed day. Asking when the next update is, or simply noticing it, helps you time your message and your follow-up.

Why direct messages beat cold DMs here

Cold DMs on social apps fail mostly because the person never asked to hear from you. The curators in a Virtual Publicist collection joined to find new artists, so a short, specific message is welcome rather than intrusive. That changes the tone of the whole exchange: the curator replies with a question instead of ignoring a stranger.

A simple message structure

  • Their name and the playlist you're writing about.
  • One sentence on why your song fits it, referring to a recent addition if you can.
  • The song title, release date and private link.
  • A thank-you, and nothing else.

Keep it under a hundred words. If you don't hear back within a week or two, one polite follow-up is enough.
