How Can You Tell a Real Playlist Opportunity From a Paid Placement?

"How do I tell a real playlist opportunity from a paid placement?" is a question every independent artist should ask before they send a track or a payment. The playlist economy is mixed. Some curators listen and say yes or no. Some sell placement and dress it up as discovery.

The short answer

A real opportunity is a person or team who programmes music they believe in, tells you how to submit, and does not sell you a slot. A paid placement is a transaction, even when the email sounds like a compliment. Virtual Publicist is built to help you find curators and editorial playlisters who actually review music, including lists of editorial playlisters open to submissions and independent Spotify playlist curators. Use that kind of list, and treat guaranteed adds as advertising, not as taste.

Signs of a real curator

  • They have a public playlist with a coherent taste, not a pile of unrelated tracks.
  • They explain how to submit, or they listen through a known platform, and they do not promise an add.
  • The follower count, if they share one, is not the only thing they talk about.
  • They may say no. That is a healthy sign.
  • They do not need your login, and they do not ask you to stream yourself.

Warning signs of a paid placement

  • The first message is praise, and the second is an invoice.
  • You are promised a number of streams, a "viral" push or a guaranteed add.
  • The playlist genre changes every week, or the artwork and title look mass-produced.
  • They want payment in gift cards, crypto or a transfer to a personal account, with no terms.
  • They ask you to change your artist name, or to route listeners in a way that breaks platform rules.

A fair way to think about money

Paying a platform to deliver your music to curators who then decide for themselves is different from buying the placement. The first is a submission fee or a software subscription. The second is advertising, and should be labelled that way in your own head, even if the seller avoids the word. Advertising is not automatically dishonest, but it will not replace a relationship with someone who likes the song.

What to do instead

  1. Pitch official editorial routes through Spotify for Artists when you have a release.
  2. Build a short list of independent curators whose playlists you would be proud to be on.
  3. Send one track, a short note, and nothing that sounds like a payment offer.
  4. Keep a record so you can see who is real the next time.

FAQ

Are all paid playlists a scam?

Not all of them are frauds, but many are a poor use of money and some can put your accounts at risk. If you cannot see the playlist, the terms and a reason the song belongs there, walk away.

Will a real curator ever charge to listen?

Some platforms charge a fee to submit, and the curator can still decline. That is different from buying the add. Read the rules.

Bottom line

Real playlist opportunities come from people who listen and decide. Paid placements sell a result. Learn the difference, protect your accounts, and spend your time on curators who already play music like yours. Virtual Publicist can help you find those people and keep the outreach clean.
