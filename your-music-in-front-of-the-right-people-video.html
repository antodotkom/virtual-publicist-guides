Your Music, In Front of the Right People (Video and Notes)

"You DM the blogs. You get left on read." That is how this 30-second video from Virtual Publicist opens, and most independent artists will recognise the feeling. The rest of the clip is about what to do instead: find the people who are already in your lane, and talk to them directly.

Why "left on read" happens

A message sent to a blog's social account competes with everything else in that inbox: friends, brands, other artists, spam. Many writers don't treat social DMs as a submission channel at all, so the message is seen, if it is seen, at the wrong moment and in the wrong place. Nothing about the song has been judged. It simply arrived somewhere that isn't built for reviewing music.

"The people already in your lane"

The useful phrase in the video is "already in your lane". For any release, a short list of people is far more likely to care than a long list of famous names: the blogs that cover your genre, the curators building playlists around your sound, the radio shows and DJs who play music like yours, local writers in your city, creators whose audience overlaps with yours, and brands looking for songs with your mood. Matching by genre, location and interest is a way of finding that short list before you write a single message.

A simple way to act on this

  1. Write down your lane. Genre and subgenre, city or region, and two or three artists you are often compared with.
  2. Find people who covered those artists recently. Recent coverage is a stronger signal than an outlet's size.
  3. Read how each person wants to receive music. Use the channel they ask for, not the one that is easiest for you.
  4. Send a short, specific message. Who you are, why this song fits what they cover, one private link, and your release date.
  5. Keep track. Note who you contacted and who replied, so you can follow up once and then move on.

Where Virtual Publicist fits

The video's point is that this list doesn't have to be built from scratch every release. Virtual Publicist groups blogs, playlist curators, radio, DJs, journalists, creators and brands into curated collections, and artists message the people inside them directly. Those contacts are on the platform because they are looking for new music, which is why the replies "come back for real". You can see how it works at https://virtualpublicist.com/.

Quick takeaways

  • A DM to a blog is often the wrong door, not a verdict on your song.
  • Start from your lane: genre, location and interest.
  • Fewer, better-matched contacts beat a long list of big names.
  • Direct, specific messages get read; generic ones get skipped.
