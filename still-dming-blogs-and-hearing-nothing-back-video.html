Still DMing Blogs and Hearing Nothing Back? (Video)

If you have sent a new song to a dozen music blogs through Instagram or X and heard nothing back, you are in very familiar territory. This 43-second video from Virtual Publicist looks at why that happens and what a better first contact looks like.

Why cold DMs to blogs go quiet

Most music writers don't treat their social inbox as a submission desk. Messages from strangers often land in a filtered folder, sit next to dozens of other requests and arrive without the basics a writer needs: who you are, what the song sounds like and why it fits their coverage. Even a great song can disappear when the message carrying it is easy to skip.

The other problem is fit. A DM sent to every blog with a large following will mostly reach people who don't cover your genre. Silence is often a sign the message reached the wrong person, not that the music failed.

What the video suggests instead

The idea in the video is simple: start with the media people who are actually looking for new music, and talk to them directly. Instead of guessing who might be open to a message, you begin from a group of blogs, curators, radio hosts and creators who are there to discover artists, then choose the ones who match your sound.

How that works in practice

  • Build your profile. Connect Spotify, SoundCloud or YouTube so your music and details are in one place.
  • Open a collection. Collections group media people by type, genre and location, so you can see who is inside before you write anything.
  • Choose by match. Decide who to contact based on genre, location or the kind of music they say they're interested in.
  • Message directly. You write each message yourself and you decide who receives it. Nothing is sent automatically.

Making the first message count

Whichever route you take, the same habits help: one personal opening line that shows you know their work, two sentences about the song, a working link and a polite follow-up if you hear nothing after a week or so. Keep the list short and well matched rather than long and generic.
