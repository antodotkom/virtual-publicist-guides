Releasing Music in December: A Week-by-Week Pitching Timeline for Independent Artists

December is a strange month to release music. Some artists avoid it completely because the calendar fills up with holiday songs, year-end lists and people switching off. Others pick it on purpose because fewer independent releases compete for the same attention. Both views have a point. What doesn't work is releasing in December on a normal schedule, because the people you want to reach are running on a holiday one.

This is a practical, week-by-week plan for a single released in December, working backwards from release day. Adjust the weeks if your date is earlier or later in the month.

Before you start: decide what kind of December release it is

There are really two kinds:

  • A seasonal song. A holiday track, a winter-themed song, anything tied to the time of year. Its window is short. It needs to be in front of people in late November and early December, not on the 23rd.
  • A regular song that happens to come out in December. Nothing about it is seasonal. You are using a quieter release week, and you accept that some outlets will hold it until January.

Be honest about which one you have. A seasonal song pitched late is wasted for a year. A regular song pitched in December can still earn coverage in the first weeks of January, when writers and hosts come back looking for new things to share.

Six weeks out: get the assets finished

Nothing gets pitched until these exist:

  • Final master and a private streaming link (an unlisted link from your distributor or a private file link that doesn't expire).
  • Cover art at full size, plus two or three press photos with a photographer credit.
  • A short bio, around 100 words, written in the third person.
  • A two- or three-sentence description of the song: what it is, who it's for, one detail that makes it different.
  • Release date, distributor and the date the song will be live on each service.

If any of this is missing at six weeks, move the release date. Rushing assets in December is how songs go out with no pitch at all.

Five weeks out: deliver to your distributor and build your list

Upload the release now. Distributors and services slow down over the holidays, and a delivery problem in mid-December can push your date. Early delivery also gives you time to pitch through the streaming services' own tools.

While that processes, build a short, specific list of people to contact. For a December single, prioritise:

  1. Blogs and online magazines that cover your genre and are still publishing in December (check their last few weeks of posts).
  2. Radio shows and podcasts that plan a few weeks ahead.
  3. Independent playlist curators whose lists you would genuinely want to be on.

Twenty well-chosen names beat two hundred random ones. Note how each person prefers to be contacted and whether they say anything about timing.

Four weeks out: pitch press first

Writers schedule ahead, and in December they schedule further ahead than usual because they plan time off. Send your press pitches now. Keep each one short, name the writer, mention one recent post of theirs that made you think of them, and include the private link, release date and one line about the song.

For seasonal songs this is the most important week. A writer putting together a holiday roundup is choosing songs right about now.

Three weeks out: pitch radio and podcasts

Weekly specialist shows often plan two to four weeks out, and holiday specials are programmed earlier than normal ones. Send the radio-ready version, the clean edit if there is one, the running time and any notes on language. Make it easy for a host to say yes without asking a follow-up question.

Two weeks out: the editorial pitch and curator outreach

Submit your song for editorial consideration through Spotify for Artists as soon as the release shows up there. Spotify's own guidance is to pitch at least seven days before release, and earlier is better, especially when staff are working holiday schedules. If you want a refresher on how that process and independent curators fit together, the Spotify playlist submission guide covers each step.

Independent curators are different. Many can only add a song once it is live, so a short heads-up now and a proper message on release day usually works better than a full pitch two weeks early.

Release week

  • Message the curators you warned, with the live link.
  • Thank anyone who covered or played the song, and share their post or show with a credit.
  • Follow up once, politely, with press contacts who haven't replied. One follow-up, not three.
  • Post the song on your own channels with a clear reason to listen now.

The weeks after: plan for January

Some replies will arrive after the holidays. That is normal. Keep a simple sheet of who you contacted, when, and what they said. In the first week of January, follow up with anyone who said "after the break", and pitch the song again to outlets that were closed in December. A regular song released on the 5th of December can still be new to a writer on the 6th of January.

A short checklist

  • ☐ Seasonal or regular release decided
  • ☐ Assets finished six weeks out
  • ☐ Delivered to the distributor five weeks out
  • ☐ Press pitched four weeks out
  • ☐ Radio and podcasts pitched three weeks out
  • ☐ Editorial pitch submitted at least seven days before release
  • ☐ Curators messaged on release day
  • ☐ January follow-ups scheduled

December doesn't reward the artists who release the most. It rewards the ones who planned around the calendar everyone else is on.
