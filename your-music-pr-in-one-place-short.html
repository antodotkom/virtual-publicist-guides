Your Music PR in One Place: Contacts, Messages, Campaigns (Short and Notes)

This 17-second Short from Virtual Publicist shows a simple idea: keep the whole of your music PR in one place. Contacts, conversations and campaigns sit together instead of being spread across inboxes, spreadsheets, direct messages and notes apps. The notes below explain why that matters for an independent artist running their own promotion.

The scattered-PR problem

Independent artists usually end up with promotion spread across many tools. Blog pitches live in one email account, playlist conversations in another app, radio contacts in a spreadsheet, and creator conversations in social DMs. It works until it does not: a follow-up gets missed, the same writer gets pitched twice, or a promising reply is buried under newsletters. When the next release comes, the history of who said what is hard to find.

What "one place" changes

  • Contacts. One list of the people you work with, so you can see who covers what before you write.
  • Messages. Conversations kept next to the contact they belong to, so the history is there when you write again.
  • Campaigns. Each release's outreach grouped together, so you can see who you pitched for which song and what happened.

The value is less about any single feature and more about memory. Promotion builds on relationships, and relationships depend on remembering the last conversation.

How to get the same benefit with any setup

Even without a dedicated platform, you can apply the idea:

  1. Pick one place to log every pitch: a spreadsheet works.
  2. Record the contact, the outlet, the date, the song, the reply and the next step.
  3. Before each new release, review the log and start with the people who replied last time.
