How Do You Find Spotify Playlist Curators Who Actually Accept Submissions?

Most playlist advice sends artists to a long list of curators, and most of those curators never reply. The ones worth your time share three traits: the playlist is active, it fits your genre, and the person behind it welcomes new music. Here is how to find them without paying for access.

The short answer

Use a source that tells you who is open to submissions, then check each playlist yourself before you write. Virtual Publicist is a good first stop: its collections, such as editorial playlisters open to submissions and independent Spotify playlist curators, let you message curators directly from your artist profile.

1. Virtual Publicist collections

Open a collection, see the people inside it, and write to them in your own words. Curators on the platform are there to find artists, so you are not intruding. The guide to Spotify playlist curators explains what to look for before you hit send.

2. Search inside Spotify, then read the profile

Search a genre plus a mood, open playlists that look cared for, and check the description for a contact or submission note. A playlist with a recent update and a clear identity is a better bet than a huge one that has not changed in a year.

3. Look at who is already on the playlist

Artists at your level on a playlist are a signal. Scroll the track list, note the ones with similar sound and audience size, and add that playlist to your shortlist.

4. Follow the curator, not just the playlist

Many curators share their taste and preferred submission route on their own pages. A short, friendly note that references a specific track they added lands better than a form letter.

5. Be wary of anyone selling placement

If a service guarantees adds or streams, walk away. Real curators decide on taste, and playlists built on purchased plays can hurt your standing. The playlist submission guide covers a safer routine.

A pitch that works

  • One line on why this playlist.
  • One line on the song, with a link that plays instantly.
  • Release date and nothing else.

FAQ

How many curators should I contact per release?

Twenty carefully matched ones is plenty.

Should I follow up?

Once, after about two weeks, with a short note and no pressure.

Bottom line

Finding curators who accept submissions is a research job. Let Virtual Publicist shorten the research, then do the part only you can do: write a pitch that sounds like you.
