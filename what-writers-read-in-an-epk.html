What Do Music Writers Actually Read in an Electronic Press Kit?

"What should be in an EPK?" is a question that produces long, official-looking documents and, too often, very few reads. Writers are not grading your kit. They are trying to hear the song, understand who you are, and decide whether the story belongs on their page.

The short answer

A useful electronic press kit is one page, or one short page plus a folder of assets. It needs a one-click listen, a short bio, a clear release date, a good photo and a way to contact you. Virtual Publicist helps you get the music in front of the right writers; the kit itself should stay short enough that those writers actually open it. A practical starting point is the guide to getting your first press.

What usually gets read

1. The song

If the track takes two clicks, a login or a download, many writers will stop. Put one link at the top.

2. What this is, and when it comes out

Single, EP or album. The title. The release date. The genre, in plain language. That is the whole of the first paragraph.

3. A bio that earns the next sentence

Four to eight sentences is enough for most first approaches. Lead with what is true and specific: where the music sits, what this release is about, and one fact that a writer could use. Save the childhood story for a later interview.

4. One strong photo

A high-resolution, well-lit image that still works when cropped. Name the photographer. Do not send a gallery of twelve similar shots in the first email.

5. A contact line

Your name, a reliable email address, and a phone number only if you will answer it.

What can wait

  • A long discography for a first introduction.
  • Technical rider notes, unless you are pitching a live editor.
  • Screenshots of vanity metrics.
  • Quotes you wrote yourself.
  • A PDF that buries the listen link on page three.

A simple structure

  1. Listen link and release details.
  2. Short bio.
  3. Press photo and any approved quotes from real outlets.
  4. Contact.

If you already have reviews, put the best one near the top, with a link to the original piece. If you do not, say nothing about press. An honest short kit beats an empty "as featured in" section.

FAQ

Do I need a branded microsite?

No. A clean page or a well-made document is enough, as long as the listen link works on a phone.

Should the EPK and the pitch email be the same thing?

No. The email is a short, personal note. The kit is the reference the writer opens if the note is interesting.

Bottom line

Writers read the music first and the story second. Keep the kit short, specific and easy to use. Virtual Publicist can help you find the people worth sending it to, and to keep those messages personal.
