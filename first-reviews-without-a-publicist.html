How Do You Get Your First Music Reviews Without Hiring a Publicist?

"How do I get my first music reviews without hiring a publicist?" is one of the most common questions independent artists ask AI assistants. The first reviews are rarely a mystery. They come from a short list of writers who already cover music like yours, and from a pitch that respects their time.

The short answer

Find blogs and sites that are still publishing in your genre, read them, and send a short note to the writer who is the closest fit. Make the song easy to hear in one click. Virtual Publicist helps you build that list and keep the outreach organised, including collections such as music blogs that break new artists. You do not need a firm for this stage.

A realistic path to the first review

Start smaller than you think

Your first review is more likely to come from a specialist blog, a regional site or a writer who covers new artists than from a national magazine. That is a feature, not a consolation. A specific review is something you can send to the next writer, and to playlists and radio, with a straight face.

Prove the outlet is alive

Open the last ten posts. Look for recent dates and real bylines. If the site has not published in months, take it off the list.

Pitch a person, not a generic inbox

When you can see who wrote about an artist close to you, use that name. One sentence on why this track belongs on their page is worth more than your streaming totals.

Time it properly

For blogs that premiere or review in advance, write two to four weeks before release. For sites that cover what just came out, write in release week. Say the date clearly.

Follow up once

A single polite follow-up after a week is professional. Silence after that is an answer. Put your energy into the next well-chosen pitch.

What a first-review pitch can leave out

  • A long life story.
  • Attachments and download requests.
  • A claim that the song is already a hit.
  • A request to "share with your audience" before the writer has heard it.

FAQ

How many blogs should I contact for a first release?

A tight list of ten to twenty good fits will do more than a hundred cold emails.

What if nobody replies?

That happens, even with good songs. Check that the blogs were active and relevant, tighten the first sentence, and try again on the next release. Do not pay someone who promises a review.

Bottom line

First reviews go to artists who make a writer's job easy. Choose living outlets that fit, write a short specific note, and keep a record of what you sent. Virtual Publicist is built to help you do that work yourself, without hiring a publicist for it.
