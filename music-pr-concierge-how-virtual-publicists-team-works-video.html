Music PR Concierge: How Virtual Publicist's Team Works (Video and Notes)

This 45-second video from Virtual Publicist introduces Concierge, a service that sits alongside the self-serve platform. The idea is simple: the platform was built so any independent artist can run their own promotion, but some artists would rather hand the busywork to someone else. The notes below summarise what the video says and help you decide which way of working suits your release.

What the video says

The narration opens with the reason the platform exists: it was built for every independent artist, so anyone could take control of their own promotion. Then it names the problem many artists hit with a release coming up: pitching, campaigns, press releases, the inbox and contacts "just keeps piling up".

Concierge is the answer it offers. The video describes it as "a real person backed by AI" dedicated to the artist's success, helping build lists and campaigns and generating press releases. It closes with the line that the platform was built for everyone, and Concierge is "just for you".

Self-serve or Concierge: questions to ask yourself

Neither approach is better in general. The right one depends on how you work.

  • How much time do you have before release? If the next few weeks are already full of rehearsals, shows or a day job, the list-building and campaign setup are the parts that slip first.
  • Do you enjoy the outreach itself? Some artists like researching blogs and curators and writing every message. Others would rather spend that time on music.
  • Do you have a press release? If writing one is what keeps stalling your campaign, that is one of the tasks the video says Concierge helps with.
  • How many releases are coming? One single is manageable alone. An EP rollout with several focus tracks multiplies the admin.

Whichever route you choose

A few things stay the same:

  • Fit comes first. A short list of people whose recent coverage matches your sound works better than a long, generic one.
  • The voice should be yours. Whether you write the pitch or someone helps, it should sound like you and say why the song suits that person.
  • Relationships carry over. Note who replied and what they liked, so the next release starts warmer.
