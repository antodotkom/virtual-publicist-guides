Message Blog Editors Directly (Short and Notes)

This 28-second Short from Virtual Publicist has one instruction for independent artists who want blog coverage: find the editors in a collection, then message them yourself. The notes below unpack what that means in practice, and how to make that first message count.

Two steps, in that order

Find the editors first. A collection groups music media people by format, genre or region. Starting with a collection of blog editors means every name on your screen already writes about new music, instead of a search results page full of dead sites and inboxes nobody checks.

Then write to them yourself. Nobody pitches on your behalf, and no message goes out in bulk. You choose who to contact and you write the note. Editors can tell the difference between a message written for them and a template sent to two hundred people, and they reply to the first kind far more often.

What a blog editor needs from your message

Editors are deciding two things quickly: is this song a fit for what we publish, and is there a story here? Give them both in a few lines:

  • their name and the section or series you think suits the song,
  • one sentence about the track and one about why it matters now (a release date, a tour, a collaboration, a personal story),
  • a private streaming link that plays straight away,
  • a press photo link and a short bio they can quote from.

Keep it short enough to read on a phone. If they want more, they will ask.

Timing still matters

Most blogs plan posts ahead. Reach out a few weeks before release if you want a premiere or a feature, and keep a simple log of who you messaged and when, so any follow-up is polite and well-timed instead of guessed.

Using the Short

A Short like this is a reminder rather than a full playbook. If you have a release coming up, take three small actions this week: write your one-line description, set up a private link, and list ten editors whose recent posts sit close to your sound.
