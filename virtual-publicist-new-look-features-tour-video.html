Virtual Publicist's New Look: A Features Tour (Video and Notes)

Virtual Publicist got a redesign in September 2026, and this 44-second tour shows the new look from the artist's side: connect a streaming profile, open collections of music media people, and message them from one inbox. Below are notes on each step and how an independent artist might use them on a real release.

Step 1: Build your artist profile from what already exists

The tour starts with connecting Spotify, SoundCloud or YouTube. Instead of filling out a long form, your profile is built from the music and details you have already published. That matters because the first thing a blog editor or curator does after reading a message is click through to hear you. A profile drawn from your real catalogue means they land on current music rather than an empty page.

A practical tip: before you connect, check that your streaming profiles are up to date. Fresh photos, a short bio and your latest release at the top make the generated profile stronger.

Step 2: Open a collection and look inside

Next, the video opens a collection. Collections group music media people by the kind of support they offer: blogs, playlist curators, radio hosts, DJs, journalists, creators and brands. Opening one shows the individual people inside, so you can read who they are and what they cover before you reach out.

Collections are not mailing lists. Nothing gets sent to anyone automatically. The point is to shorten research, which is usually the slowest part of doing your own music PR: hours of searching for who covers your genre, then guessing whether they still accept music.

Step 3: Message people directly, from one inbox

The last part of the tour is messaging. You write to the people you chose, and replies come back to the same inbox, so a conversation with a curator does not get lost among other email. The people in collections are on the platform because they are looking for artists to support, which changes the tone of the first message: you are not cold-DMing a stranger on social media and hoping they check their requests folder.

How to use this on your next release

  • Pick one collection that matches the release. A dance single and an acoustic EP need different first stops.
  • Shortlist five to ten people. Read what each one covers and keep only those who fit your sound.
  • Write a short, specific message. One line about why the song suits them, a link, and a sentence of context is enough.
  • Track replies and follow up once. Keeping conversations in one place makes this easy.

Who the redesign is for

The new look is built for independent artists, managers and small labels running their own campaigns across all genres and territories. If you have been doing music PR from spreadsheets and social media inboxes, the tour shows a calmer way to work.
